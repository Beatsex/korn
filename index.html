Morph between two patches

A patch sets every control. Your own recordings stay as the source, except with voice and oscillator patches.

Loop range keeps the scanning playheads inside a part of the sample, starting at Position; negative Scan runs backwards. Sync puts grains on the tempo grid nearest to the density. Drag anywhere in a cell to change it; swipe up or down to scroll. Move your finger further down while dragging for fine steps. Double-tap resets, tapping the name locks it against Randomize. The arrow next to Hold folds the keyboard away.

The window is the volume shape of every grain. Plateau keeps grains full and dense, Pluck gives sharp attacks, Swell plays every grain backwards in loudness. Skew moves the peak; size jitter makes every grain a different length (the faint outlines).

Octave shimmer sends some grains an octave up (mostly) or down. Pitch glide bends every grain while it plays. Drift detunes each grain by a few cents for a chorus-like width. Random pitch and shimmer follow the scale under Sound.

Every grain can get its own low-pass filter. Filter spread gives each grain a different brightness around the cutoff, up to three octaves either way.

Shape the spectrum. Harmonic sieve keeps only the overtones of the root note set under Grain › Sound. In the spectrum view you wipe frequencies away with your finger; with Erase off you paint them back in. Any spectral control delays the sound by about 50 ms.

Tune to scale pulls every frequency onto the nearest note of the scale. Harmony adds a pitch-shifted copy of the whole spectrum at the chosen interval.

Still frame holds the spectrum of this moment. Spectral sustain lets every frequency ring on and fade slowly. Diffuse scrambles the phases into a whisper.

Both need source B, chosen under More › Source. Morph blends the two spectra; Vocoder gives A the formants of B.
